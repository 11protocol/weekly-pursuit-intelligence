# 11Protocol Weekly Pursuit Intelligence

### Federal pursuit intelligence for GovCon capture and business development teams

Most federal procurement information tells you **what happened**.

The question for a capture or BD team is often:

> **What should we be paying attention to right now — and why?**

**11Protocol Weekly Pursuit Intelligence** is an experimental intelligence brief designed to help answer that question.

Each brief focuses on a specific federal contracting sector and uses federal procurement data to identify contracts and procurement signals that may deserve attention before an opportunity becomes obvious.

The goal is not to provide another database of opportunities.

The goal is to **surface signals, prioritize what appears worth watching, and translate those signals into potential actions for capture and business development teams.**

## What the briefs are intended to show

Depending on the sector, a brief may identify signals such as:

* Contracts approaching important transition points
* Potential recompete or recompetition opportunities
* Funding changes
* Contract extensions
* Growth or continuation signals
* Acquisition or administrative risk
* Contracts showing signs of distress or instability
* Opportunities that may warrant earlier capture attention
* Strategic accounts or contracts worth monitoring

The brief then attempts to translate those signals into **practical pursuit or monitoring considerations**.

The underlying idea is simple:

> **Don't just tell me what contracts exist. Help me understand which ones deserve my attention.**

## Why I'm sharing these

This is an **early-stage experiment**, and I am deliberately putting the work in front of people who actually understand federal capture and business development.

I am trying to determine:

* Whether this type of intelligence is genuinely useful
* Which signals are most valuable
* Whether the prioritization makes sense to practitioners
* What information is missing
* How the briefs could fit into an actual capture or BD workflow

**I am especially interested in honest feedback.** If something doesn't make sense, isn't useful, or would not influence your actions, that's just as valuable to know as what works.

### A note on the reports

These are experimental briefs generated from publicly available federal procurement information.

They are intended as **decision-support and research tools**, not as authoritative statements about future procurements or guaranteed opportunities.

Always independently verify underlying procurement information before making capture, business development, teaming, or other business decisions.
